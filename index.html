Project 01
What it is

A drug screening platform that tests multiple CFTR mutations against multiple candidate drugs in one 96-well assay, measuring channel function, membrane proximity and intracellular pH together in the same living cells.

I took it from an idea through optimisation and formal validation (Z′-factor, SSMD, signal-to-background) to something four institutions across three countries now use.

Hits are confirmed independently by patch-clamp, Western blot and co-IP.

Project 03
What it is

Showed by high-content functional assay that the BK channel activator GoSlo-SR-5-6 acts as a direct CFTR potentiator, rather than working indirectly as assumed.

It also synergises with ETI (elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor) to increase anion transport in both wild-type and mutant CFTR. That could matter for patients who respond poorly to current modulator therapy.

Validated by patch-clamp electrophysiology.

Project 04
What it is

Built and characterised surface-charged liposomal formulations to get hydrophobic CFTR-relevant compounds across the membrane.

Characterised by dynamic light scattering and zeta potential, with delivery profiled in reporter cell lines using the same quantitative imaging approach as the main platform.

Co-supervised with UCL Chemistry, so it runs across two departments.

Project 05
What it is

Built automated image-segmentation and scoring pipelines in Python, MATLAB and ImageJ with UCL Computer Science and Advanced Research Computing.

Throughput went up by around 50% and analysis time down by around 40%. More importantly, scoring stopped depending on who was doing it.

This later extended into ML-guided molecular docking across multi-million-compound libraries.

The Instrument

From "I didn't know it existed"
to the institute's expert

Three years, one microscope, a third of a year lost to downtime. Nobody asked me to take this on.

Institute-wide point of contact, which is me
September 2023, day one
Zero prior knowledge

I had never heard of the Molecular Devices ImageXpress. I didn't know it existed, what it did, or where it came from. My entire PhD would depend on it.

Year one, the breakdown
A year of downtime, and no engineer

The only microscope available to me went out of action for roughly one third of a full year. There was no technician support and no engineer available. So I taught myself to diagnose it, then took over the vendor relationship: organising and leading site visits, presenting findings to Molecular Devices field engineers, and escalating to account managers and department heads until it was fixed.

Meanwhile
The PhD still got done

Through all of it I completed my PhD data generation on schedule, while building the CFTR platform, running five projects at once and training 11 students.

2026, now
The institute's primary technical resource

I am the institute's primary technical point of contact and trainer for the platform. Technicians, PIs, researchers and students route their instrument questions to me rather than to the vendor. I am currently training the four technicians responsible for supporting it before I leave, and my PI records me running key protocols on video as reference training material for future students.

“The world expert on the ImageXpress.”

How my PI describes me, in writing. Others across the institute say the same.
